Cost versus accuracy

Cheapest per call is not automatically cheapest per correct answer. Where does each model sit?

Up and to the left is better. Jev and OpenAI form the frontier: no model is both cheaper and more accurate than either. Clef is more expensive and less accurate than Jev. Per correct answer, Jev costs about $0.02 per thousand, OpenAI $0.03, Flash $0.04 and Clef $0.09.

Calibration gap is how far "90% sure" is from being right 90% of the time; 0.03 is honest, 0.15 is bluffing. Accuracy at 80% automation: if you auto-act on the 80% most confident answers and send the rest to a human, this is how often the automated part is right.

Speed

The typical wait, and the bad-day wait your timeout has to cover.

OpenAI has the fastest typical call. Jev has the narrowest spread: its 95th percentile is 70 ms above its median. Clef has the widest: one call in twenty takes over three seconds. No HTTP errors or timeouts for any model.

Is the gap real?

With 842 questions, small gaps can be luck. We re-shuffled the results 2,000 times; if the 95% range crosses zero, call it a tie.

Three verdicts, no other wording: a range that stays on one side of zero is a real gap; one that crosses zero is too close to call. The p-values are McNemar tests on the cases where the two models disagreed. Jev, OpenAI and Clef agree with each other on about 91% of questions; Flash agrees with the others on 80 to 85% of questions.

Method

What was tested, how, and what to be careful about.

Setup

  • 300 cases across five domains: support triage, invoice routing, security incident severity, tool-call versus clarify, and an out-of-scope guardrail set. Each case carries one to three questions of three types: yes/no, choice, and a 1–4 score.
  • Gold labels were hand-written and tagged clear, borderline, ambiguous or out-of-scope. 85 cases are paraphrases of another case with the same gold; a subset is also presented as JSON or as a long input.
  • Same payload to every model: state plus questions. OpenAI Decisions gets the predicate/choice/score translation and a stringified state, since it rejects object states.
  • Protocol: seeded shuffle, models run round-robin per case to balance time of day, 1 request per second, 60 s timeout, retries only on 429/5xx. Latency is client-measured. Cost is computed from each response's reported usage at list price.
  • Stats: paired bootstrap with 2,000 resamples for accuracy gaps, McNemar on disagreements, n reported everywhere.

Caveats

  • One set of labels. Gold labels were written by us and may carry bias. Tier tags and per-slice reporting are the mitigation.
  • Sample size. 300 cases is enough to separate Flash from the rest, not enough to rank Jev, OpenAI and Clef with confidence.
  • Text only. Clef and OpenAI accept images, Jev does not, so images were excluded to keep the comparison fair.
  • Pricing uses published list prices. Clef-flash's input rate was ambiguous in the docs; the $0.09 per million figure was used.
